Presentation Guide: AttackAware PolyIoM
A simple guide to the 24 slides


THE MAIN IDEA IN ONE MINUTE
===========================

Face and voice systems store a coded version of your face or voice. We call this
a template.

If a password is stolen, you change it. You cannot change your face or voice. So
the template is locked with a secret key. If the template is stolen, we change
the key, and the stolen copy should stop working. This is called cancelling (or
revoking) the template.

Our system locks the template in two steps:

  - Step 1: a secret maths formula (a polynomial) scrambles the face or voice
    data and makes it smaller.
  - Step 2: a second secret key turns the result into a short list of numbers
    (IoM hashing).

Our main findings:

  - Making the data smaller with a secret key (Step 1) is what allows a stolen
    template to be cancelled.
  - The polynomial also hides the real face or voice from an attacker.
  - This hiding costs a little accuracy. So there is a trade-off.


WORDS USED IN THE SLIDES
========================

  - Template: the coded version of a face or voice that the system keeps.
  - Key: a secret code, like a password, used to lock the template.
  - Error rate (EER): how often the system makes a mistake. Lower is better.
  - pp (percentage points): the gap between two percentages. From 1% to 3% is a
    gap of 2 pp.
  - Linkability (Dsys): can someone tell that two locked templates belong to the
    same person? 0 means no, 1 means yes.
  - Similarity score (cosine): how close the attacker's copy is to the real face
    or voice. 0 means nothing alike, 1 means the same.
  - Firm result: a result we are confident about, not a matter of luck.


PART 1: THE QUESTION
====================

Slide 1: Title
--------------

The title gives the main finding: locking and shrinking the data with a secret
key is what makes a stolen template cancellable. We fixed all the test rules
before looking at the test data.

Say: This talk is about keeping face and voice data safe, and about which step
lets us cancel a stolen copy.


Slide 2: Abstract
-----------------

The whole paper in four points. One: what the system is. Two: it recognises
voices well, making mistakes only about 2% of the time on people it has never
seen. Three: the protection costs about 2 points of accuracy, but an attacker
cannot rebuild the real voice. Four: without the secret shrinking step, a stolen
template can never be cancelled.

Say: The system works, it hides the real biometric, and it can be cancelled.
Each of these has a small, measured cost.


Slide 3: The problem
--------------------

A protected template must pass four tests. It must still recognise people. A
thief must not be able to rebuild the face or voice from it. Two copies made
with different keys must not be linkable. And after a theft, a new key must make
the old copy useless. Most studies skip the last test. We do it.

Say: Most studies check three of the four tests. We also check the fourth: can a
stolen template really be cancelled?


Slide 4: The system and our three questions
-------------------------------------------

The data goes through four steps: face or voice data, secret polynomial, second
secret key, stored template. We ask three questions. Can the simplest version
(without Step 1) be cancelled? No, never. What makes cancelling work? Shrinking
the data with a secret key. What does the polynomial add? It hides the real
voice much better, but costs a little accuracy.

Say: Cancelling works because of the secret shrinking step. The polynomial adds
extra hiding.


Slide 5: What this paper adds
-----------------------------

We tested the system more fairly than usual. We removed one part at a time to
see what each part does. We attacked every version in the same way. We tested
cancelling. We compared everything with an unprotected system.

Say: Our main contribution is a fairer way to test systems like this one.


Slide 6: Background
-------------------

Other studies often choose their settings using the same data they test on. They
rarely test each part separately. And they claim the data is safe without
actually attacking it. We avoid all three problems.

Say: We test the things other studies usually take for granted.


PART 2: HOW WE DID IT
=====================

Slide 7: The face and voice models
----------------------------------

We used two well-known, ready-made models: FaceNet for faces and ECAPA for
voices. They turn a photo or voice clip into a list of numbers. We did not
change or retrain them, so any difference we see comes from our protection.

Say: We used standard models without changes, so the results show the effect of
the protection only.


Slide 8: How a template is made and checked
-------------------------------------------

First, the secret polynomial shrinks the voice data from 192 numbers to 48.
Next, a second key turns these into a short list of positions, such as 3, 1, 4.
To check a person, the system counts how many positions match. Enough matches
means the person is accepted.

Say: The stored template is just a list of positions. Checking a person means
counting how many positions match.


Slide 9: Choosing the key and the settings
------------------------------------------

We tried thousands of keys on one group of 50 people and kept the best. Then we
tried 80 settings on another group of 42 people and kept the best. Both were
then locked so they could not be changed.

Say: All choices were made on separate people, then locked before testing.


Slide 10: Splitting the people into groups
------------------------------------------

People were split into four separate groups: 50 for choosing the key, 42 for
choosing the settings, 58 for the main test, and 110 from a different voice
dataset for a final check. The test groups were not used until everything was
locked.

Say: No one in the test groups was seen while we were building the system. This
keeps the results honest.


Slide 11: How we measured
-------------------------

We measured five things: how often the system makes mistakes, how often it
accepts the right person, how linkable the templates are, how often an attacker
gets in, and how often a stolen template still works after the key is changed.
We repeated each calculation 2,000 times on reshuffled data to see how certain
each result is.

Say: We only call a result firm when the reshuffling shows it is not due to
luck.


Slide 12: Tools and data
------------------------

This slide lists every software tool and dataset, with exact versions. Anyone
can repeat the work and get the same numbers.

Say: Everything is recorded, so the study can be repeated exactly.


PART 3: RESULTS
===============

Slide 13: The locked settings
-----------------------------

For voice, the system met its target easily. For face, no setting reached the
voice target, because the face model itself already makes about 3.5% mistakes.
So we set an easier face target. We did this before seeing any test data.

Say: We set an easier target for face, openly and before testing, because the
face model alone could not reach the harder one.


Slide 14: Testing on new people
-------------------------------

On 58 new speakers, the system made mistakes 1.93% of the time. Protection made
voice accuracy about 2 points worse, and we are confident about this cost. For
face, the data was not enough to measure the cost clearly.

Say: Protection costs about 2 points of accuracy on voice. On face, we cannot
tell yet.


Slide 15: Testing on a different voice dataset
----------------------------------------------

We took the locked system and tested it, unchanged, on 110 speakers from a
different dataset. It made mistakes 2.80% of the time, with no clear drop in
quality. One target was missed: it wrongly accepted strangers 0.377% of the
time, against a goal of 0.1%.

Say: On completely new data, the system still worked well. We report the one
target it missed.


Slide 16: What the polynomial costs
-----------------------------------

We compared three versions: with the polynomial, with a simpler secret step
instead (a linear map), and with neither. The polynomial causes most of the
accuracy loss, about 1.2 points on voice.

Say: The polynomial is the main cost. The next slide shows what we get for it.


Slide 17: What an attacker can and cannot do
--------------------------------------------

We imagined the strongest attacker, who knows all the keys. This attacker can
always create a fake input that the system accepts, for every version. But with
the polynomial, the attacker's copy looks very little like the real voice: a
similarity score of 0.222, compared with 0.490 for the simpler version and 0.913
with no polynomial.

Say: The attacker can trick the system, but cannot recover the real face or
voice. That is why cancelling is important.


Slide 18: Can a stolen template be cancelled?
---------------------------------------------

We changed the keys 80 times. Without the shrinking step, the stolen template
still worked every time (80 out of 80): cancelling failed. With the polynomial,
it usually stopped working, but in 12 of the 80 cases many stolen copies still
worked, and we do not yet know why. With the simpler linear step, cancelling
worked every time. An earlier, smaller test had suggested the polynomial was
clearly worse here. With more keys, that difference disappeared, so we withdrew
that claim.

Say: Without the secret shrinking step, cancelling never works. With it,
cancelling works in most cases.


Slide 19: Summary of results
----------------------------

One table shows all four tests and how strong the evidence is for each. The
bottom line: the shrinking step makes cancelling work, the polynomial hides the
real biometric, and choosing between the polynomial and the simpler step is a
trade-off.

Say: Here are all the results in one place.


PART 4: WHAT IT MEANS
=====================

Slide 20: Discussion
--------------------

First, the polynomial hides the real face or voice, but it does not stop a fake
input from being accepted. Second, cancelling is the test that best shows the
difference between designs. Third, other studies should attack simple versions
too, test each part separately, and try many keys.

Say: The most important test is the one most studies skip: cancelling.


Slide 21: Which design to choose
--------------------------------

The polynomial hides the real face or voice better, but it is a little less
accurate and cancelling sometimes fails. The simpler linear step is more
accurate and cancelling always works, but it hides less. Choose the polynomial
if losing the real face or voice is the biggest worry. Choose the simpler step
if accuracy matters most. Always include a secret shrinking step.

Say: Neither choice is best in every way. Pick based on what you need to protect
most.


Slide 22: Limitations
---------------------

The study has limits. The test groups are small. Face was not tested on a second
dataset. Only one kind of attack was tried. We do not yet know why some keys
fail to cancel. The face model itself limits how accurate face can be.

Say: These limits tell us how far to trust the results. None of them changes the
main findings.


Slide 23: Conclusion
--------------------

The secret shrinking step makes cancelling possible. The polynomial hides the
real face or voice. Choosing between them is a trade-off. Anyone can check every
number in this study with one command.

Say: Shrink the data with a secret key so templates can be cancelled. Add the
polynomial if hiding the real biometric is worth a small loss in accuracy.


Slide 24: Declarations
----------------------

An AI assistant helped with some code, the figures and the writing. The main
design and results came first. Our code and results are shared. Only public data
was used, and we attacked only our own templates.

Say: We are open about how AI was used and what we share.


THREE THINGS TO REMEMBER
========================

  - The secret shrinking step is what makes a stolen template cancellable.
  - The polynomial hides the real face or voice from attackers.
  - This hiding costs a little accuracy, so it is a trade-off.


QUESTIONS PEOPLE MAY ASK
========================

Why not just use the simpler linear step? Because it hides less of the real face
or voice, and a stolen face or voice can never be replaced.

Were the results tuned to look good? No. All settings were chosen on separate
people and locked before testing.

Can others repeat this? Yes. Everything is recorded, and one command re-checks
every number.
